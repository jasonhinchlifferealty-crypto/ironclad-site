The market in four lines

  • Sales. 7,790 homes sold in the first ten months, 6.8% fewer than in 2025. October was down 15.9%.
  • Prices. The benchmark price, the price of a typical home, was $339,400 in October. That is 1.2% above a year earlier and 3.6% below May.
  • Supply. Inventory is 5.1 months, up from 4.1 a year ago. Buyers have the widest October selection in more than five years.
  • Rates. The Bank of Canada raised its rate to 2.50% on October 28 and said more increases may be needed.

The forecasts

We graded seven published forecasts for New Brunswick against the data. None issued before April 2026 scored above a simple no-change assumption. They expected steady interest rates to release pent-up demand. Rates rose instead.

Grades for 2026 are provisional until December data is published. The full scorecard, with the date each forecast was issued and the rubric used, is in the report. Our own calls are logged and graded by the same rubric in later editions.

What it means

  • Sellers. Price to this fall's sold comparables. The May benchmark was 3.8% above today's.
  • Buyers. More choice and less urgency. A half-point change in mortgage rate moves the payment on a typical home by about $88 a month.
  • Owners staying put. The five-year gain of 58.8% is intact. We expect flat values, not a reversal.

One reading to expect. The year-over-year price change is likely to turn negative by April or May 2027, because May 2026 set a high base. It will describe an adjustment that has already happened.
