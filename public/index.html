    Who you'll be dealing with

    We answer the phone.

    Before real estate, I spent fifteen years building a commercial insurance brokerage — the kind that ran on analytics while the industry ran on golf. We once inventoried every pound of plastic in a manufacturer's warehouse to calculate their maximum possible burn. The sprinkler system everyone had called inadequate turned out to be more than enough, and their premiums came down. That is what evidence does: it replaces opinions with numbers, and numbers are negotiable in your favour.

    I sold that company for the same reason I'd started it — on the strength of the data. Then came the easiest decision on this page. I'd been coming to this corner of New Brunswick since I was a kid, so when it was time for a fresh start I brought my family to see it. They fell in love with the area, and that was that.

    The way I worked then is the way I work now. My line in insurance was, "You're not always going to like what I say, but you can damn well trust it." Pricing a home is a risk calculation. I run it on comparable sales, absorption, and days-on-market data, and I'll show you the printout — whether or not it says what you were hoping.

    The rest of the file: philosophy at the University of Toronto, published essays, trained musician, trained martial artist, about a thousand hours of volunteer work, and one hot-wing eating title that made the server question whether I was human.
